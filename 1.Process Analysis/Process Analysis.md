
# Process Analysis 

## The Core Definition
Reflecting on the theoretical foundations of process analysis, I see it as more than just a diagnostic tool. It is a critical lens for understanding how an organization operates. According to ABPMP International, process analysis systematically identifies and examines activities to evaluate how effectively and efficiently operations align with organizational goals. This concept resonated with me because it highlights the importance of establishing a shared, clear picture of the "actual" state before jumping to solutions. It showed me how process analysis proactively targets improvement opportunities—whether that means optimizing efficiency, mitigating operational risks or navigating new regulatory demands. 

## Strategic Purpose
In June 2025, Amazon announced Wellspring, a generative AI mapping system, together with a new AI-powered demand-forecasting model (Amazon, 2025a, para. 3). Wellspring combines satellite imagery, road networks, building footprints and past-delivery data to pinpoint precise drop-off points in complex apartment clusters, while the forecasting model blends sales history, weather and holiday data to place inventory more accurately across fulfilment centres.
 
I chose this artefact because it shows process analysis being applied to a real end-to-end delivery process. Amazon gathered information from customer instructions and historical delivery outcomes to evaluate how effectively its current process met its accuracy and speed goals, then used that analysis to guide redesign. This mirrors the lecture's point that analysing information systems and data flow is a core step in preparing to improve a process.

## Application and Opportunities
Announced in October 2025, Blue Jay is a ceiling-mounted robotics system that coordinates multiple arms to pick, stow and consolidate items, collapsing what were previously three separate stations into one workspace (Amazon, 2025b, para. 4). Alongside it, Project Eluna is an agentic AI model that gives fulfilment-centre operators natural-language, data-driven recommendations for avoiding bottlenecks.
 
I selected this artefact because it demonstrates process analysis leading directly to redesign: Amazon identified a bottleneck created by multiple handover points and consolidated it into a single, more efficient station. This connects strongly to the lecture's guidance on asking the right bottleneck and human-involvement questions, and shows how continuous, real-time analysis (via Eluna) supports the "when to perform analysis" concept of ongoing monitoring rather than waiting for a single triggering event.

## Techniques for analysing Information Systems 
In my study of Techniques for Analysing Information Systems, I learned that process analysis requires examining system event logs, performance metrics, and data handoffs. Watching process mining applied directly to transactional datasets brought these theoretical frameworks to life.  The video demonstrated how software ingests raw event logs to calculate exact cycle times, exposing severe idle delays and bottlenecks between process steps. Furthermore, it illustrated root-cause analysis by isolating specific data attributes that trigger repeated re-work.  This directly reinforced my understanding that process analysis is not a matter of guesswork; it relies on objective, system-generated evidence. Seeing cycle-time spikes and their underlying causes uncovered through automated discovery proved how identifying performance gaps leads directly to data-driven, justified TO-BE redesigns. It reassured me that the analytical techniques taught in class are actively powering modern enterprise engineering

## Context within Business Process Management 
Published in September 2025, this practitioner guide explains that business process analysis reveals bottlenecks and wasted resources by breaking workflows into clear, manageable steps, and it recommends prioritising customer-facing and high-volume processes before tackling smaller ones (Kazinik, 2025, para. 2).
 
I chose this artefact because it reinforced the core definition and strategic purpose of process analysis in plain, accessible language, which helped me consolidate the more technical lecture content. Its advice to prioritise customer-facing, high-impact processes directly matches the lecture's "Prioritize the Process" criteria, confirming that this is a widely accepted practice beyond the classroom and giving me a quick reference I can return to when analysing my own case studies later in the term.
